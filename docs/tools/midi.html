About Bandolibre

Bandolibre is an open-source digital Argentine bandoneon, built by L'Atelier du Bandonéon Libre. It uses Hall-effect sensors and MIDI to bring the bandoneon's playing feel to a fully open, buildable instrument.

This page is the configuration tool: connect your Bandolibre over USB-MIDI to view the keyboard live, tune the bellows and pedal response, and adjust firmware properties, no install needed.

MIDI bandwidth budget

This reading is raw MIDI byte throughput in both directions, not a hard link limit. Rough, reasonable budgets to keep in mind:

5-pin DIN MIDI (the original serial link): a fixed 31.25 kbit/s (~3.9 kB/s). A 3-byte note message takes ~1 ms to transmit, so dense traffic (fast pitch bend, CC sweeps, chords) can visibly lag behind the notes that trigger it. A safe sustained budget is well under that ceiling — roughly 15-20 kbit/s (~2-2.5 kB/s) keeps timing tight.

USB-MIDI, Full Speed (this board's transport, 12 Mbit/s link, shared with the console): messages are packed into 4-byte USB-MIDI event packets and polled every 1 ms USB frame. That's a huge step up from DIN-5 - a comfortable budget for glitch-free performance is on the order of a few hundred kbit/s (tens of kB/s), leaving the link mostly idle.

USB-MIDI 2.0 / UMP (Full Speed or High Speed): richer per-note/high-resolution packets cost more bytes per event, but even a busy MPE performance stays a small fraction of the link. Budgeting in the low Mbit/s range is generous.
